## 5 prompting techniques that actually work

These aren't tricks — they're frameworks. Use them intentionally and you'll see a measurable difference in output quality. 1. Role assignment Tell Claude who to be. It changes the tone, depth, and frame of the entire response. Example prompt "You are a senior marketing strategist reviewing this campaign brief. Give me your honest critique — what's strong, what's weak, and what's missing." 2. Format specification Tell Claude exactly how you want the output structured. Don't leave it to guess. Example prompt "Give me your answer as a numbered list, max 5 items, each with one sentence of explanation. No intro paragraph." 3. Chain of thought Ask Claude to think out loud before giving the final answer. Better for complex problems. Example prompt "Walk me through your reasoning step by step before you give me your recommendation." 4. Few-shot examples Show Claude an example of what you want before asking it to do the thing. Example prompt "Here's an example of the tone I want: [paste example]. Now write a product description for [product] in the same style." 5. Constraint setting Tell Claude what NOT to do. Negative constraints are often more powerful than positive ones. Example prompt "Write this email. Do not use corporate jargon. Do not start with 'I hope this email finds you well.' Keep it under 100 words."

## Where Claude actually saves you time

Here are the use cases where people consistently report the biggest time savings. These aren't theoretical — they're what people are actually doing with Claude right now. 📧 Difficult emails Paste the situation, ask for a draft. Saves 20–30 min of staring at a blank screen. 📋 Meeting prep Feed it the agenda and background, ask for questions to ask and things to watch out for. 🧹 Editing your own writing Paste your draft, ask for specific improvements. Keep your voice, fix the weak spots. 🔍 Research summaries Paste articles or reports, ask for the key points and what it means for your situation. 🧠 Thinking out loud Describe a problem you're stuck on. Use Claude as a sounding board to think it through. 📝 SOPs and templates Describe a repeatable process, ask Claude to turn it into a clean, reusable template. 💡 The pattern: any task that's repetitive, that requires a first draft, or that involves synthesizing a lot of information is a Claude use case.

## Prompting is a skill. Here's how to practice it.

The difference between someone who gets mediocre AI output and someone who gets great output isn't talent — it's iteration. They tweak, they refine, they experiment. Here's the iteration loop that works: Step 1: Write your first prompt Don't overthink it. Get something out there. Step 2: Evaluate the output What's right? What's wrong? What's missing? Be specific. Step 3: Give targeted feedback Not "that's bad" — tell it exactly what to fix. "Too formal." "Make the opening punchier." "Cut the last paragraph." Step 4: Repeat until it's right Most great AI outputs take 3–5 rounds of back and forth. That's normal — not a failure. Real iteration in action You Write a LinkedIn post about my promotion. C Excited to share that I've been promoted to Senior Manager at [Company]! Grateful for the opportunities and the incredible team... You Too generic. Make it more personal — mention that it took 3 years, and add something genuine about what the team means to me. Less corporate. C Three years ago I wasn't sure this was the right move. Today I got promoted to Senior Manager — and honestly the team is the whole reason... See how the second version is actually usable? That's iteration at work.

## Where Claude falls short — be honest with yourself

No tool is perfect. Knowing where Claude struggles is just as valuable as knowing where it shines. Here's the honest rundown: ❌ Real-time information Claude's knowledge has a cutoff date. Don't ask it for today's stock price, yesterday's news, or anything time-sensitive without giving it the information yourself. ❌ Math and calculations It can do basic math, but it can make arithmetic errors on complex calculations. Always verify numbers. Use a calculator for anything important. ❌ Hallucination Claude can confidently state something that isn't true — especially specific facts, citations, stats, or names. If something sounds slightly off, verify it. This is the big one. ❌ Your niche domain knowledge Claude knows a lot but not everything. For highly specialized or proprietary information, it's only as good as what you give it. Feed it context, don't assume it already knows. 💡 The golden rule: Claude is a thinking partner and a first draft machine — not a source of truth. Verify anything that matters.

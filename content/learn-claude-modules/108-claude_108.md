## "Custom AI" gets thrown around a lot. Here's what it actually means.

You've probably heard phrases like "we fine-tuned our own AI" or "train a custom model on your data." These sound impressive, but they cover a huge range of very different things — from a five-minute setup to a multi-million dollar undertaking. This module breaks down: 🪜 The customization ladder — from prompting to full training, and everything between 💬 Prompting vs. fine-tuning — the most common confusion, cleared up 📚 What fine-tuning actually requires — data, cost, and effort realistically 🏗️ Training from scratch — why almost nobody actually does this 🎯 How to choose — a practical framework for matching the approach to the need By the end, you'll be able to cut through "custom AI" marketing claims and understand what's actually being offered.

## There isn't one way to 'customize' AI — there's a ladder

Think of customizing AI behavior as a ladder with increasing effort, cost, and control as you climb: 1 Prompting Just describing what you want in plain language — instructions, examples, context — every time you ask. Effort: minutes · Cost: free-to-low 2 System prompts / custom instructions Setting standing instructions once so they apply automatically across a conversation or account, without repeating yourself each time. Effort: minutes · Cost: free-to-low 3 Retrieval (giving the model your data to reference) Connecting documents, databases, or files so the model can look up your specific information rather than relying purely on training memory. Effort: hours-to-days · Cost: low-to-medium 4 Fine-tuning Taking an existing trained model and further training it on your specific examples, so its behavior shifts to match a particular style, format, or domain. Effort: days-to-weeks · Cost: medium-to-high 5 Training from scratch Building an entirely new model from raw data with no starting point — essentially what Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google do. Effort: months-to-years · Cost: millions to billions 💡 Key insight: The vast majority of "custom AI" you'll encounter in business — probably over 95% of it — happens at rungs 1-3. Fine-tuning is real but much less common than the marketing suggests, and training from scratch is essentially never done outside a handful of major AI labs.

## Prompting vs. fine-tuning: the distinction that matters most

This is the confusion worth clearing up first, since it comes up constantly: prompting and fine-tuning are fundamentally different operations. Prompting Fine-tuning What changes Nothing in the model — you just phrase your request well each time The model's internal weights are adjusted through additional training Where it lives In your instructions/conversation Baked permanently into the model itself Reusability Has to be included every time (or set as a standing instruction) Behavior persists automatically, no repeated instructions needed Data needed None, or a few examples in the prompt itself Typically hundreds to thousands of examples Cost/effort Low — mostly your time to write good instructions Higher — data prep, training runs, evaluation Analogy Prompting is giving directions. Fine-tuning is teaching a new habit. Prompting is like giving someone turn-by-turn directions each time they drive somewhere — effective, but you're providing guidance every trip. Fine-tuning is more like actually training them until the route becomes second nature, so they no longer need directions at all. One is a repeated instruction, the other is a lasting behavior change. 💡 Practical rule: If good prompting can achieve the behavior you want, you almost never need fine-tuning. It's the more expensive, more complex option — worth reaching for only when prompting genuinely can't get you there.

## What fine-tuning actually takes, realistically

When fine-tuning genuinely is the right call, here's what it actually involves — useful to know so you can evaluate whether a project or vendor claim is realistic: 📊 A real, quality dataset Typically hundreds to thousands of high-quality example input/output pairs that demonstrate exactly the behavior you want. Garbage or inconsistent examples produce a worse model, not a better one — data quality matters more than raw quantity. 💰 Real compute cost Running additional training passes over a model costs real money and time, scaling with model size and dataset size. Far cheaper than training from scratch, but not free or instant. 🔬 Evaluation, not just training After fine-tuning, you need a way to actually measure whether the new model performs better on your task than the original — skipping this step means you can't tell if the fine-tuning helped or quietly made things worse in some areas. 🔁 Maintenance over time A fine-tuned model is a snapshot. If the base model gets updated, or your needs change, you may need to redo the fine-tuning process rather than it updating automatically. ⚠️ Red flag to know: If someone claims they can "fine-tune you a custom AI" in an afternoon with no discussion of your data quality or evaluation plan, be skeptical — that's more likely a prompting/retrieval setup being marketed as fine-tuning.

## Training from scratch: why almost nobody actually does this

At the far end of the ladder is training an entirely new foundation model from raw data — no existing model to start from. This is what companies like Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google actually do, and the scale involved explains why virtually no one else attempts it. Training a modern frontier model requires: Massive, carefully curated datasets — often trillions of words of text Enormous compute infrastructure — thousands of specialized processors running for weeks or months Costs typically in the tens to hundreds of millions of dollars, sometimes more for the largest models Deep research expertise in model architecture, training stability, and safety 💡 Why this matters practically: When you hear "we built our own AI model," it's worth asking what that actually means. In the overwhelming majority of cases, it means building on top of an existing model — through prompting, retrieval, or at most fine-tuning — not training a new one from the ground up. That's not a lesser achievement, it's just accurately describing what actually happened.

## A practical framework for choosing the right approach

Pulling this together into a decision process: 1️⃣ Start with prompting, always Write clear, detailed instructions with a few good examples. This solves the majority of customization needs on its own, at minimal cost. 2️⃣ Add retrieval if you need specific knowledge If the AI needs to know your specific documents, policies, or data — connect that information rather than trying to cram it all into a prompt or, worse, assuming fine-tuning is required. 3️⃣ Consider fine-tuning only for a persistent, specific behavior gap If prompting and retrieval genuinely can't achieve a specific, consistent behavior — a very particular output format at scale, for example — and you have quality training data, fine-tuning becomes worth the investment. 4️⃣ Training from scratch: essentially never, for almost everyone Unless you're a major AI lab with massive resources, this isn't a realistic option — and it's very rarely actually necessary even for ambitious custom AI projects. 💡 The mindset worth having: Climb the ladder only as far as you actually need to. Most impressive "custom AI" results come from thoughtful prompting and good data connections — not from the most technically complex option available.
